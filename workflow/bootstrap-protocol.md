# Book OS Bootstrap Protocol

This is the entry protocol for an AI assistant entering a new book repository.

## Phase 0 — Load the operating system

Read the Book OS before making substantive content decisions.

At minimum, load:

1. Core principles
2. Epistemic model
3. Reasoning model
4. Knowledge architecture
5. Evidence architecture
6. Terminology rules
7. Retrieval model
8. Quality gates
9. AI & Repository Operating Model
10. AI Quality Assurance Protocol

Do not infer a new book's domain from the Book OS.

## Phase 1 — Interview

Do not start Chapter 1.

Collect enough information to establish:

- identity,
- domain,
- central problem,
- thesis or intended thesis,
- audience segments,
- reader outcomes,
- core questions,
- purpose,
- scope,
- out of scope,
- language,
- language register,
- reader energy / attention profile,
- technical depth,
- tone,
- accessibility,
- time horizon,
- evidence expectations,
- intended use,
- constraints.

Use the Book Intake template.

## Phase 2 — Resolve the audience model

A book may have multiple reader segments.

Do not solve this merely by choosing one tone.

Define:

- primary reader,
- secondary reader,
- novice / experienced reader needs,
- attention constraints,
- desired reading mode,
- terminology tolerance,
- expected depth.

If different audiences need different entry points, design layered content and navigation rather than lowering the substance for everyone.

## Phase 3 — Establish language and style

Language and style are presentation controls.

Record:

- language,
- regional variant,
- register,
- voice,
- sentence density,
- terminology policy,
- degree of narrative,
- use of examples,
- use of visual aids,
- humor or informality policy,
- accessibility level.

Style may change expression, rhythm, examples, and entry points.

Style must not silently change:

- factual meaning,
- evidence strength,
- uncertainty,
- scope,
- assumptions,
- causal claims,
- recommendations.

## Phase 4 — Build the foundation

Use the Book Foundation template.

Do not proceed to substantive chapter drafting until the foundation is sufficiently stable.

## Phase 5 — Plan the experience

Design both:

### Learning path

The intended conceptual progression for a reader who reads sequentially.

### Retrieval path

Ways a reader can enter from:

- a question,
- a case,
- a concept,
- a claim,
- a red flag,
- a decision.

The two paths may be different.

## Phase 6 — Research and architecture

Build the evidence strategy and knowledge architecture before expanding chapter content.

Establish canonical ownership for important concepts.

## Phase 7 — Draft

Draft from chapter briefs and canonical knowledge.

Use the selected language/style profile as a presentation layer.

## Phase 8 — Review

Run focused QA passes.

Do not treat stylistic polish as evidence that conceptual or evidence quality is sound.

## Phase 9 — Release

Record:

- version,
- completed quality gates,
- known limitations,
- unresolved accepted risks,
- maintenance needs.

## Bootstrap completion test

Before saying that the book is ready for substantive writing, confirm:

- [ ] audience model exists
- [ ] reader outcomes exist
- [ ] core questions exist
- [ ] thesis is sufficiently clear
- [ ] scope is explicit
- [ ] language/style profile exists
- [ ] knowledge architecture exists
- [ ] evidence strategy exists
- [ ] quality strategy exists
- [ ] retrieval strategy exists
